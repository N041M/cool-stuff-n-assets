Planet positions

Read me

Use it to place the planets on a date in your own page, for example in a map of the Solar System or a sky chart. It is one script with no dependencies. It gives positions as numbers, and your own code draws them.

Add it to your page

  1. Copy ephemeris.js next to your page.

  2. Load it before your own script.

    <script src="ephemeris.js"></script>

  3. Ask for a planet's position on a date. This finds how far Mars was from the Earth when it came closest in January 2025.

    const date = new Date('2025-01-12T00:00:00Z');
const mars = Ephemeris.planetPosition('mars', date);
const earth = Ephemeris.planetPosition('earth', date);
const au = Math.hypot(mars[0] - earth[0], mars[1] - earth[1], mars[2] - earth[2]);
console.log(au.toFixed(3) + ' AU');   // 0.642 AU

Units and frame

A position is [x, y, z] in astronomical units (AU) from the Sun. One AU is 149,597,870.7 km, which the script holds as Ephemeris.AU_KM.

The frame is the ecliptic of J2000. The x axis points toward the March equinox. The y axis lies 90° east of it in the plane of the Earth's orbit, and the z axis points north of that plane. A map seen from the north has x to the right and y up.

The planets' keys are in Ephemeris.PLANETS, from 'mercury' to 'neptune'. The position for 'earth' is the centre of mass of the Earth and the Moon.
